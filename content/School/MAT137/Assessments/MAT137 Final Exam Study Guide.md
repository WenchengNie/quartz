## Definitions and theorems to know (so far)
- **L'Hôpital's rule** with all four hypotheses (see the note). If $\lim\frac{f'}{g'}$ doesn't exist, the rule says nothing.
- Indeterminate forms: $\frac00,\ \frac\infty\infty,\ 0\cdot\infty,\ \infty-\infty,\ 1^\infty,\ 0^0,\ \infty^0$.
- Local extremum ⇒ critical point (not the converse). Global extrema on $[a,b]$: compare critical points and endpoints.
- Rolle's theorem and the MVT (hypotheses: continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$).

## Top traps
1. Applying L'Hôpital to something that isn't $\frac00$ or $\frac\infty\infty$ (e.g. $\frac{2}{0}$).
2. Concluding "the limit doesn't exist" because $\lim\frac{f'}{g'}$ doesn't exist ($\frac{x+\sin x}{x}\to1$).
3. Using L'Hôpital on $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ is circular.
4. Exponential forms: take $\ln$ first, compute the limit of $g\ln f$, then exponentiate.
5. Optimization: $f'(c)=0$ alone doesn't prove a global max/min. Check endpoints or the sign of $f'$, and state the domain.
